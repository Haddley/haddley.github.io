**Summary.** I explore how the roles of business analysts, developers, and support staff change when coding agents maintain solutions instead of just building them. I conclude that most human roles will shift from performing manual tasks to deciding what work should be done and verifying that the agents completed it correctly.

## What I did
- I identified the shift from project-based agent work to ongoing maintenance.
- I analyzed how specific roles like support and analysis change in an agent-centric world.
- I examined how segregation of duties remains necessary in regulated environments.
- I mapped the transition of human roles toward decision-making and verification.

## Claims
- The agent that builds a solution can also maintain it.
  > "In Coding Agents That Never Leave? I argued that coding agents may not just build solutions and hand them over: the agent that builds a solution can also maintain it."
- The ticket remains as a record and approval even if an agent writes it.
  > "So my guess is: yes, for kinds of fix that are approved in advance, but the ticket does not disappear."
- An agent cannot decide which business proposal to back or own the assumptions.
  > "What an agent cannot do is decide which proposal the business should back, or own the assumptions inside it."
- An agent that builds and runs a solution collapses the traditional separation of duties.
  > "An agent that builds and runs a solution collapses it."
- One agent builds while a different agent or person approves to maintain audit logs.
  > "The likely answer is not to drop the rule, but to split the work: one agent builds, a different agent or a person approves, and the audit log records who did what."
- Developers will review more than they write while testers write the checks.
  > "Analysts may matter more, developers review more than they write, testers write the checks, and platform engineers run the services the common rules depend on."

## Outcome
I concluded that if agents stay to maintain what they build, human roles will move toward deciding and checking while the common rules serve as the benchmark.

## Caveats
Some of these changes have not happened yet and some may not happen.
