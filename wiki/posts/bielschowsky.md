**Summary.** I published an interactive page to demonstrate how my right superior oblique palsy affects my vision and why I maintain a specific head posture. The page visualizes the limitations of standard corrective lenses and the specific rotational components that only a head tilt can address.

## What I did
- I built an interactive HTML page to visualize my binocular double vision.
- I developed an OpenXR project to measure and correct retinal image rotation.
- I mapped my personal measurements to the Bielschowsky head-tilt test.
- I created a web-based port of my vision correction tools for various headsets.

## Claims
- Standard glasses cannot correct the rotational component of my vision.
  > "What it does not carry — what no pair of glasses can carry — is the *rotational* part, because a prism bends light up, down or sideways and no optic rotates a retinal image."
- My head tilt is a functional response to a specific muscle failure.
  > "That last sentence is the whole reason for my head posture."
- The rotation of my eyes is partially corrected by the vestibular system.
  > "When the head rolls, the otoliths command ocular counter-roll — the eyes rotate against the head to keep the world upright, recovering about 15% of the roll."
- The correction I apply to my eye's position is actually an inward movement.
  > "The third component is the one I had been drawing as nothing, and it turns out to go the way most people guess wrongly: **inward**."
- The rotation curve is based on fifteen hand-dialled corrections.
  > "The rotation curve is fitted to fifteen hand-dialled corrections across four sessions in a single day."
- The assumption that vertical deviation scales away from head level remains untested.
  > "The vertical component is measured at head level only and *assumed* to scale away from it — that assumption is drawn hatched throughout the page and has never been tested."

## Outcome
I built an interactive tool that visualizes the relationship between my eye's rotation, my head posture, and the limitations of my current prescription.

## Caveats
The data is based on a single person and the assumption that vertical deviation scales away from head level has never been tested.
